### 21–100 Pattern
⚠️ **Important:** Dutch numbers are said "ones THEN tens" (like German):
- 21 = **éénentwintig** (one-and-twenty)
- 35 = **vijfendertig** (five-and-thirty)

## Telling Time ⏰

### Key Vocabulary
| Dutch | English |
|-------|---------|
| Hoe laat is het? | What time is it? |
| Het is ... | It is ... |
| uur | hour / o'clock |
| half | half (BEFORE the next hour!) |
| kwart over | quarter past |
| kwart voor | quarter to |
| 's ochtends | in the morning |
| 's middags | in the afternoon |
| 's avonds | in the evening |
| 's nachts | at night |

### Time Examples

| Time | Dutch | Literal Translation |
|------|-------|-------------------|
| 1:00 | één uur | one o'clock |
| 2:15 | kwart over twee | quarter past two |
| 3:30 | half vier ⚠️ | half four |
| 4:45 | kwart voor vijf | quarter to five |
| 5:10 | tien over vijf | ten past five |
| 6:20 | tien voor half zeven | ten before half seven |
| 7:35 | vijf over half acht | five past half eight |
| 8:50 | tien voor negen | ten to nine |
| 12:00 | twaalf uur / middag | twelve o'clock / noon |
| 00:00 | middernacht | midnight |

### ⚠️ The Tricky "Half" Rule
This is the **#1 mistake** learners make!

In Dutch, **half** refers to the NEXT hour:
- **Half drie** = 2:30 (NOT 3:30!)
- **Half elf** = 10:30 (NOT 11:30!)

Think of it as "halfway TO three" = 2:30

| Dutch | English | Abbreviation |

### Dates
Dutch dates use the format: **day-month-year**
- 5 juni 2026 = June 5, 2026
- "Welke datum is het?" = "What's the date?"
